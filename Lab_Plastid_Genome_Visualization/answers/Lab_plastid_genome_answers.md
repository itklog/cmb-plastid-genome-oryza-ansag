### Part E. Questions to Answer

1. **What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?**

   The chosen plant is *Oryza sativa* (Asian rice, Japonica Group), and its plastid genome accession number is **NC_001320.1**.

2. **What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?**

   The total length of the plastid genome is **134,525 base pairs (bp)**.

3. **Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**

   Yes. The plastid genome has a typical quadripartite structure consisting of one Large Single-Copy (LSC) region, one Small Single-Copy (SSC) region, and two Inverted Repeat (IR) regions.

   * **LSC:** The largest region of the genome and occupies most of the circular map.
   * **SSC:** The smaller single-copy region located between the two IR regions.
   * **IRa:** One of the two inverted repeat regions bordering the SSC.
   * **IRb:** The other inverted repeat region bordering the opposite side of the SSC.

4. **Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.**

   Three genes located in the LSC region are:

   * `rbcL` – encodes the large subunit of Rubisco.
   * `psbA` – encodes the Photosystem II reaction center protein D1.
   * `rpoB` – encodes the beta subunit of the plastid-encoded RNA polymerase.

5. **Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.**

   One example is `ndhF`, which encodes a subunit of the NADH dehydrogenase-like complex involved in photosynthetic electron transport. Other genes in this region include `ccsA`, `rps15`, and `ndhD`.

6. **Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?**

   Examples include `rpl2`, `rpl23`, `rps7`, `rps12`, and the ribosomal RNA genes such as `rrn16`, `rrn23`, `rrn4.5`, and `rrn5`.

   Yes. Genes located within the duplicated portion of the IR regions can occur twice in the plastid genome because IRa and IRb contain homologous sequences. Therefore, the same gene can be present in both IR regions.

7. **Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.**

   * **Gene Name:** `rbcL`
   * **General Biological Function:** The `rbcL` gene encodes the large subunit of Rubisco, which catalyzes the fixation of carbon dioxide during the Calvin cycle of photosynthesis.

8. **What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?**

   The direction of the arrows indicates the orientation of the genes and the direction in which they are transcribed. Genes facing opposite directions are located on opposite DNA strands and are therefore transcribed in opposite directions.

9. **Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.**

   The GC content is not completely uniform across the plastid genome. It varies across different regions, with differences observed among the LSC, SSC, and IR regions. These variations show that the nucleotide composition is not identical throughout the genome.

10. **Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?**

   A graphical plastid genome map makes the overall organization of the genome easier to understand. It allows us to quickly see the locations and orientations of genes, the LSC, SSC, and IR regions, gene duplications within the IRs, and patterns in GC content. This is more convenient for analyzing genome structure than looking at the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations.
